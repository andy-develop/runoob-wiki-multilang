Machine Learning - Customer Segmentation

In today's data-driven business world, understanding customers is the key to success.

However, when your customer base reaches tens of thousands or even millions, manually analyzing each customer's characteristics and behavioral patterns becomes impractical.

At this point, machine learning techniques, especiallyunsupervised learningclustering algorithms within it, become a powerful tool.

Customer segmentation, also known as customer segmentation, has the core goal of dividing the large customer base into several subgroups with similar characteristics. This is like an experienced store owner who no longer treats customers as a vague whole, but can clearly identify different groups such as value-conscious homemakers, tech enthusiasts keen on new products, and high-end customers who value service experience. By adopting targeted marketing, service, and product strategies for different groups, companies can significantly improve operational efficiency and customer satisfaction.

This article will guide you step by step through a complete customer segmentation hands-on project. We will use the classic K-Means clustering algorithm to analyze a simulated retail customer dataset, from data understanding to model evaluation, ultimately obtaining segmentation results with business insights.

Understanding Cluster Analysis and K-Means Algorithm

Before starting the hands-on practice, we need to understand the core tools we are about to use.

What is Cluster Analysis?

Cluster analysis is an unsupervised learning method. Unlike supervised learning (such as predicting house prices or identifying cat and dog images), clustering algorithms do not have pre-labeled "correct answers" (i.e., labels). Its task is to explore the inherent structure of data, automatically grouping similar data points into the same group (called a "cluster"), while making data points in different groups as dissimilar as possible.

A simple analogy: Imagine you have a basket of mixed fruits containing apples, oranges, and bananas. The task of the clustering algorithm is to automatically pile together fruits that are similar in shape, color, and size without anyone telling you the category names.

How the K-Means Algorithm Works

K-Means is one of the most commonly used and intuitive clustering algorithms. "K" represents the number of clusters we want to divide the data into. Its working principle can be summarized in four steps:

  1. Initialization: Randomly select K data points as the initial "cluster centers" (centroids).
  2. Assignment: Calculate the distance from each data point to each centroid (usually using Euclidean distance), then assign each point to the cluster of the nearest centroid.
  3. Update: Recalculate the centroid of each cluster (i.e., the mean of all points in that cluster).
  4. Iteration: Repeat steps 2 and 3 until the positions of the centroids no longer change significantly, or the preset number of iterations is reached.

The flowchart below clearly illustrates this process:

Algorithm Core Points：

  • Distance Metric: Usually, Euclidean distance is used to measure the similarity between data points; the closer the distance, the higher the similarity.
  • Centroid: Represents the "average point" or center of a cluster.
  • Objective: Minimize the sum of squared distances from data points to their centroid within each cluster (called "within-cluster sum of squares" or Inertia).

Step 2: Data Exploration and Preprocessing

Before applying the algorithm, we must first understand the data and do the "cleaning" work properly.

1. Exploratory Data AnalysisDiscover patterns initially through visualization and statistics.

Example

# Visualize feature distributions
fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 3, figsize=(15, 4))

2. Data PreprocessingClustering algorithms are very sensitive to the dimension (units) of features. The numerical ranges of annual income (tens of thousands) and age (tens) differ greatly, which will seriously affect distance calculation and cause the income feature to dominate the clustering results. Therefore, we need to performfeature standardization, which scales each feature to a standard normal distribution with mean 0 and variance 1.

Example

# Select features for clustering
features = ['Age', 'Annual_Income_(k$)', 'Spending_Score']
X = df[features].copy()

# Feature standardization
scaler = StandardScaler()
X_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X) # fit calculates mean and variance, transform applies the transformation
X_scaled_df = pd.DataFrame(X_scaled, columns=features)
print("First 5 rows of standardized data:")
print(X_scaled_df.head())

Step 3: Determining the Optimal Number of Clusters (K Value)

K-Means requires us to specify the K value in advance. How to choose a reasonable K? We use two classic methods:

1. Elbow MethodPlot the within-cluster sum of squares (Inertia) corresponding to different K values. Inertia decreases as K increases; we look for the inflection point of the curve (like an elbow), after which the benefit (decrease in Inertia) from further increasing K becomes smaller.

Example

inertia = []
K_range = range(1, 11) # Test K from 1 to 10

for k in K_range:
    kmeans = KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=42, n_init='auto') # n_init='auto' is a usage in newer versions
    kmeans.fit(X_scaled)
    inertia.append(kmeans.inertia_) # Get the Inertia under this K value

# Plot the elbow method graph
plt.figure(figsize=(8,5))
plt.plot(K_range, inertia, 'bo-')
plt.xlabel('Number of Clusters (K)')
plt.ylabel('Within-cluster Sum of Squares (Inertia)')
plt.title('Elbow Method: Choosing the Best K Value')
plt.xticks(K_range)
plt.show()

2. Silhouette Coefficient MethodThe silhouette coefficient measures the similarity of a data point to its own cluster (cohesion) and its separation from other clusters. Its value ranges from -1 to 1,the higher the better, indicating better clustering performance.

Example

silhouette_scores = []
K_range = range(2, 11) # The silhouette coefficient requires at least 2 clusters

for k in K_range:
    kmeans = KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=42, n_init='auto')
    cluster_labels = kmeans.fit_predict(X_scaled)
    score = silhouette_score(X_scaled, cluster_labels)
    silhouette_scores.append(score)

# Plot the silhouette coefficient graph
plt.figure(figsize=(8,5))
plt.plot(K_range, silhouette_scores, 'go-')
plt.xlabel('Number of Clusters (K)')
plt.ylabel('Silhouette Coefficient')
plt.title('Silhouette Coefficient Method: Choosing the Best K Value')
plt.xticks(K_range)
plt.show()

Combining the elbow method graph (inflection point) and the silhouette coefficient graph (peak), we assume and determineK=5is a good choice.

Step 4: Applying K-Means for Clustering

Train the model using the selected K value and assign cluster labels to each customer.

Example

# Use K=5 to train the final model
final_k = 5
kmeans_final = KMeans(n_clusters=final_k, random_state=42, n_init='auto')
df['Cluster'] = kmeans_final.fit_predict(X_scaled) # Add cluster labels to the original dataframe

# View the number of customers in each cluster
cluster_counts = df['Cluster'].value_counts().sort_index()
print("Distribution of customer counts per cluster:")
print(cluster_counts)

# View feature means per cluster (original scale)
cluster_profile = df.groupby('Cluster')[features].mean().round(2)
print("\nAverage feature values per cluster:)
print(cluster_profile)

Transform abstract cluster labels into intuitive insights.

plt.figure(figsize=(10, 6))

Step 6: Model Evaluation and Application Recommendations

Evaluation: In addition to the silhouette score, you can check whether the sample distribution within clusters is balanced, and combine business logic to judge whether the segmentation is reasonable.

Application Recommendations：

  • Precision marketing: Push high-end new products and exclusive events to the "Ideal VIP type" (Cluster 3); send discount coupons and group-buying information to the "Value-seeking type" (Cluster 2).
  • Product development: Design fashionable products with strong social attributes for the "Young potential type" (Cluster 4).
  • Customer service: Provide detailed product data and security guarantees to the "High-income cautious type" (Cluster 0) to alleviate their concerns.
  • Resource allocation: Allocate more customer service and marketing resources to high-value customer segments.

Summary and Further Exploration

Through this hands-on case, you have fully experienced the process of customer segmentation using the K-Means algorithm:Data preparation -> Exploratory analysis -> Preprocessing -> Determine K value -> Train model -> Analyze results。

Key Points Review：

  • Clustering is an unsupervised learning method used to discover inherent groupings in data.
  • Feature standardizationis a key step before using distance-based clustering algorithms.
  • Elbow method和Silhouette scoreare practical tools for determining the optimal number of clusters.
  • The interpretation of clustering results mustbe combined with business knowledgeto produce real value.
