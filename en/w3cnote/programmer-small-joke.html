Little humor only programmers can appreciate

Categories Programmer jokes

1. A wife called her programmer husband: "On your way home from work, buy ten buns. If you see a watermelon vendor, buy one." That night, the husband walked in the door holding a single bun... The wife yelled angrily: "Why did you only buy one bun?!" The husband, terrified, mumbled: "Because I really did see a watermelon vendor."

2. A programmer went for an interview. The interviewer asked: "You only graduated two years ago, so how do you have three years of work experience?!" The programmer replied: "Overtime."

3. A baby was very good at math. By age 2, he could count from 1 to 10. Later, I told him that 0 is smaller than 1. Today we were eating dumplings, and I said: "Baby, count how many dumplings you want to eat?" "0, 1, 2, 3." While saying this, he picked up a dumpling, "This is the 0th one." His mother roared: "The next generation is still destined to be a programmer!"

4. A programmer was very interested in calligraphy and decided to achieve something in it after retirement. So he spent a fortune buying top-quality scholar's four treasures. One day, after dinner, he was suddenly struck with inspiration. He ground some ink, laid out paper, lit fine sandalwood incense, quite in the style of Wang Xizhi and with the bearing of Yan Zhenqing. After a moment of concentration, he splashed ink and boldly wrote a line: hello world.

5. Question: Which of Emperor Kangxi's sons do programmers hate the most? Answer: Yinsi. Because he is the Eighth Prince (bug).

6. A programmer had three children and named them Ctrl, Alt, and Delete. If they misbehaved, the programmer just had to press them all at once and everything would be fine...

7. If you don't succeed at first, call it version 1.0.

8. Today at the company I heard the most devastating insult possible: "You're nothing but a wild pointer without an object!"

9. Worry not that ahead there are no kindred spirits, for there will always be bugs following you.

10. If the world makes programmers destitute, programmers will make the world silent.

11. At dusk at the open-source inn, entering the stack I know not the way back. Debugging has no end, I stray deep into the code. Step, step, I find countless bugs.

12. With cold looks I face ten thousand lines of code, bowing my head I'm willing to be a code-writing ox. How much sorrow can you bear? Just like finishing debugging code and then having requirements changed.

13. Bugs know the season, they appear when going live. They sneak in with the night wind, silently 404. Dark clouds cover the wild paths, a single window burns bright alone. At dawn I look at the company, there sits a lone programmer.

14. Cheng XX was in a car accident and became a vegetable. The doctor said she had only a one in ten thousand chance of survival, and an even slimmer chance of waking up. Her colleagues and family didn't give up, and knowing Cheng XX's obsession with testing, they read to her every day: "The module you tested was rolled back after going live..." A miracle happened. Cheng XX woke up and her first words were: "Are you sure that module was tested by me?"

15. Two programmers,

  • A: Hey buddy, I'm a bit strapped for cash lately. Can you lend me some money?
  • B: Sure, how much do you need?
  • A: Is a thousand okay?
  • B: We're like brothers! Let me round it up for you. Here's 1024, take it.

16. A programmer drowned while swimming at the beach. Before he died, he desperately called for help. There were many lifeguards on the beach, but no one saved him. Because he kept shouting "F1!" "F1!" and no one knew what "F1" meant.

17.

  • 1) A programmer's life is truly painful... A requirement gets made and changed, and a month passes by; wail~ the requirement gets changed and adjusted again, and a quarter passes by; wail~
  • 2) Do you know what the most painful thing for a programmer is? It's when the program isn't finished and the requirements change again;
  • 3) Do you know what the most, most painful thing for a programmer is? It's when the system was barely finished and the entire plan gets changed;
  • 4) Do you know what the most, most, most painful thing for a programmer is? It's when the system is done and the damn client runs away;
  • 5) Do you know what the most, most, most, most, most painful thing for a programmer is? It's when the damn client comes back, and the program has been deleted!

18. One day a programmer met God.

  • God: Young man, I can grant you one wish.
  • Programmer: I wish the Chinese national team could make it into the World Cup again.
  • God: That one! That one is hard to arrange. Why don't you make another wish!
  • Programmer: Alright! My next wish is to be able to rest more than 6 hours every day.
  • God: I think I'll just let China make it into the World Cup.

19. After being a programmer for several years, I got tired of it and wanted to change careers. So I started furiously hammering the Enter key every day.

20. Question: A pair of programmer lovers sit facing each other working. What do you guess they're doing? Answer: Object-oriented programming.

21. Ten years, life and death, both blur. Writing programs until dawn. Thousands of lines of code, where do bugs hide? Even if it goes live, what then? Requirements change in the morning, heartbreak by evening. Leaders get new ideas every day, changes every day, busy day and night. We look at each other in silence, only tears streaming down. Every night where lights flicker in the distance, programmers, working overtime again, workaholics.

22. The farthest distance in the world is when I'm in the if and you're in the else. Though we often appear together, we never execute in tandem.

23. Ten lines of code, nine warnings, eight errors, yet you dare to claim mastery in seven days, learning it in six days. Even across the five lakes and four seas, there's never been such a half-hearted project manager. Simply first-class despicable.

24. Programmer: My first question is, for my second and third questions, can you answer only with 'yes' or 'no'?

  • Boss: "OK!"
  • My second question is, if my third question is whether I can get a raise, then can your answer to my third question be the same as your answer to my second question?
  • Boss: ......

25. "The Rainy Alley of the Programmer" Opening MyEclipse, alone I wander in the long, long and lonely rainy alley, hoping to meet a girl debugging code just like me. She has the messiness of requirements, the absurdity of design, the sorrow of testing. In the code she laments, lamenting and wandering; she wanders in this lonely rainy alley.

26. A female classmate was eating in the cafeteria when a programmer sidled up: "Excuse me, can I talk to you? I haven't spoken to a girl in a month."

27. A classmate asked me what software outsourcing is. After explaining a few sentences and he still didn't get it, I thought for a moment and asked: You know what a construction contractor is, right? Suddenly it clicked!

28. I was coding when a colleague who just got back from the hospital looked totally miserable. I asked him what was wrong. He said: "I've got rheumatoid arthritis, and I'm afraid it'll be passed down to the next generation." I looked at him with a face full of question marks: "Who said rheumatoid arthritis is hereditary?" He looked at me with surprise: "Isn't it inherited from a class?"

29. A true warrior dares to face the bleak warnings and look straight at the dripping errors.

30. A thief snuck into an IT company intending to rob it, but there were always people around, so he had to wait and wait and wait. The result was that there was always a large group of people working overtime. After a month, the thief finally found a chance to slip out. His accomplice asked: "Where have you been?" The thief said: "I spent a month in an IT company." The accomplice said: "Must have gotten quite a haul, right?" The thief smiled bitterly: "Don't even mention it. After thirty all-nighters, I can now write programs."

31. A colleague had been having a lot of bugs recently. I pretended to care and asked him how many bugs he had. He answered: 10. A few days later, I asked again. He answered: 10. I said: "So many days have passed, how is there no change at all?" He said: "What do you mean no change? Before it was in decimal, now it's in hexadecimal."

32. I'm really puzzled by the word "inn" (stack). Does the one who checks in later have to check out first?

33. If life deceives you, find 50 programmers and ask them why they code; if life makes you want to die, find 50 programmers and ask them if their bugs are fixed yet; if you feel financially strained, find 50 programmers and ask them if their salaries have been raised; if you feel bored with life, find 50 programmers and ask them what they do all day!

34. The key factor that determines whether a programmer changes jobs is the current salary of their former colleagues~

35. The most frustrating thing for a programmer is: you stay up all night writing an elegant source file, and then a colleague with terrible code style modifies it without signing it, so everyone thinks you wrote that mess...

36. Last night I dreamed I got the martial arts manual of the Nine Yin Scriptures. I opened the first page and, damn, it clearly read: "hello world!"

37. A front-end engineer said he went to a dating site to find a girlfriend. A friend asked: "Did you find one?" The engineer said: "I found a bug on their page..."

38. Ten years, life and death, both blur. Baidu thrives, Google dies. Weibo is hot, Twitter has no place to lament. Even if we met, we wouldn't recognize each other. MSN is dead, WeChat is wild. Renren and Jiayuan rejoice returning home. JD.com's Liu, great at picking up girls. Taobao exists, overseas shopping is busy. Videos fight each other, forums rely on perverts, Momo helps people get into bed. Talking until Ma Yun's heartbreak, Yu'e Bao, I'm afraid it won't live long.

39. When a programmer is called to fix a computer, the correct response is: "Oh, sorry, I'm a programmer, I don't know how to fix computers. Actually, I'm only good at breaking computers. Honestly, all the problems on your computer were caused by us programmers."

40. Everything up to today is my own doing. Being unable to find a girlfriend is just being unable to find a girlfriend; it has nothing to do with anyone else. The bitter civilization of my programmer career has shaped my shy, introverted personality and my disregard for worldly affairs, leading to today's precarious state. Today I am willing to bear all the consequences. In fact, I'm very grateful to you for letting me fall today, rather than in my still-uncivilized future. I must sort myself out, face it calmly, and fix the bugs! This code farmer has written a life full of bugs. I have let down my boss and my boss's boss.

41. I applied for a "Network Attack Research Manager" position at a large IT company. The interviewer asked me: "Why do you think you're suitable for this job?" I said: "I hacked into your system and sent myself the interview invitation."

42. My wife asked me to teach her programming because she wanted to see how web pages are made. So I taught her to make some simple little programs with JQuery and PHP. After writing some Hello World programs, my wife said: "Programming isn't that hard after all, but you programmers are so pretentious. Whether it's front-end or back-end code, there are '$' symbols everywhere. Are you guys obsessed with money or what?"

43. A student posted on Renren: "Can't find object can't find object can't find object can't find object can't find object." I replied: "Please make sure the object exists, and correctly spell its name and pathname." Another student replied: "If you really can't get the object, just declare a base class!"

44. Last night I dreamed that my boyfriend was shopping with another woman. In the dream, my first reaction was to check the source code... After debugging for a long time, I couldn't figure out why that woman was displayed instead of me. Finally, with tears in my eyes, I commented out that woman. When I ran it again, my boyfriend was shopping alone... When I woke up, I made an "囧" face and stayed stunned for a long time... 囧rz

45. A qualified programmer would never write a program like "Destroy the Earth." Instead, they would write a function called "Destroy Planet" and pass the Earth in as a parameter.

46. If your friend is an IT worker, don't go Dutch at the year-end dinner. Treat him instead—offering to pay is a good choice. Never mention year-end bonuses, traveling abroad, buying a house or a car in front of him. Life isn't easy; we need to support each other and move forward.

47. When the designer saw the interface implemented by the program, they felt a mix of emotions—like marrying their daughter to a small-time gangster, and in the end she gave birth to a bunch of little hooligans.

48. Real programmers like to also sell popcorn on the side. They use the heat emitted by the CPU to make popcorn, and they can tell what program is running by the speed of the popcorn popping.

49. Wishing everyone in the days ahead: may the guys be as robust as Oracle; may the girls be as beautiful as Win7; may your romantic luck be as frequent as IE getting infected; may your wallet grow as large as Gmail's storage; may your promotions keep pace with Microsoft's patches; may chasing girlfriends be as rampant as Trojans; may life be as happy as after a computer reinstall; and may writing programs and typing code be as passionate as chatting.

50. The boss told a joke, and the whole office burst into laughter—some wiping tears, some clutching their stomachs, some pounding the table. Only Xiao Yuan wasn't laughing. While laughing, I asked him, "Why aren't you laughing?" He said, "I've already resigned!"

51. The four things programmers hate most: writing comments, writing documentation, others not writing comments, others not writing documentation...

52. A soldier fell in love with a princess. The princess told him that if he would stand under her balcony for 100 consecutive nights, she would accept him. So the soldier did it—he waited one day, two days, three days... until the ninety-ninth day, then he left. Why didn't the soldier hold on for one more day? The answer is heartbreaking—because the soldier was a programmer, and he had been counting from 0.

53. One night after work, I was walking home when a police officer on patrol came toward me. Suddenly he shouted: "Stop!"

  • Officer: How many bytes does the int type take?
  • Me: 4.
  • Officer: You can go now.
  • I was very puzzled.
  • Me: Why ask such a question?
  • Officer: Walking the streets late at night, looking shabby and miserable—you're either a thief or a programmer...
