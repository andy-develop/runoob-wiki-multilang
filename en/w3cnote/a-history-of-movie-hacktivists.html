"The Social Network" is a 2010 film directed by David Fincher, adapted from the 2009 best-selling book "Facebook: A Secret History of Sex, Money, Genius, and Betrayal". It tells the story of Facebook founder Mark Zuckerberg, who, as a Harvard student, created the social networking site Facebook that changed global online communication, and the story of betrayal behind it.

13. "The Core" (2003)

20. "The Conversation" (1974)

Produced by the American Coppola Film Company, written and directed by Francis Ford Coppola. This film is Coppola's masterpiece of critical realism and is regarded as one of the best films of the 1970s. The writer-director's tendency is very clear: the theme condemns a modern society that has lost privacy, making people more lonely and suspicious of each other, accelerating interpersonal tension and human alienation. The scene in which the protagonist tears down his house to search for a listening device has become a classic. The film won the Palme d'Or at the 1974 Cannes International Film Festival; received three Academy Award nominations; and won the National Society of Film Critics Best Director Award.

21. "Hackers" (1995)

Besides "The Net" starring Sandra Bullock, another science-fiction crime film from 1995 with a future cyberworld theme, "Hackers", is also quite excellent. From today's perspective, the film's cast can be considered strong—although at the time they were all new actors: Johnny Lee Miller and Angelina Jolie co-starred. But perhaps because of an overly imaginative take on the identity of hackers, both performances are a bit stiff. Watching the fantasies in the film become reality one by one, and the film's actors go on to become leading Hollywood stars, is indeed a delight.

22. "Tron" (1982)

"Tron" is a 1982 Disney science fiction film. Directed by Steven Lisberger and starring Jeff Bridges and others, with Taiwanese animation company Wang Film Productions responsible for most of the hand-drawn coloring work. The film also released a sequel in 2010—"Tron: Legacy".

23. "War Games" (1983)

The crowd-pleasing, entertaining sci-fi film "WarGames" accurately captures the "war-weariness" psychology of most audiences. With the love story of protagonist David and his girlfriend Jennifer running throughout, it handles the scenes about nuclear weapons and war very cleverly, allowing the film to avoid a so-called "tragic ending," thus winning the welcome and affection of audiences.

24. "Sneakers" (1992)

This film showcases the modernization of computers and is eye-opening. Cases of computer crime are often seen in the news, but shocking events like those depicted through detailed description in this film make people strongly feel the potential crises that the future development of technology may bring to humanity. The story's appeal lies in the meticulous and detailed description of the terrifying functions of that "black box," as well as the wisdom of the main characters led by Robert Redford.

25. "Live Free or Die Hard" (2007)

This is a convention specially created by the "Die Hard" series: John McClane always shows up at the wrong place at the wrong time, then is forced into a series of troubles. It was an unspecified July 4th, which happened to be a weekend. Although New York police officer John McClane was on vacation, he didn't even have time to say goodbye to his college-age daughter Lucy before he was called away by a phone call from headquarters. He received a mission: arrest hacker Matt Farrell and take him to the FBI for questioning... This looks like an ordinary case any way you look at it, but don't forget John's natural knack for attracting trouble. As soon as John arrives at Matt's apartment, it's as if some mechanism has been triggered, and all kinds of chaos follow one after another.

26. "GoldenEye" (1995)

As the 17th James Bond film, "GoldenEye" came into being through great hardship. Because 1989's "Licence to Kill" became the worst-performing Bond film at the box office, the prospects for a new installment were worrying. The same year, Australian media group Qintex acquired Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer/United Artists, which faced a merger with Pathé. Meanwhile, Danjaq, the parent company of EON Productions, sued MGM/UA because the latter, without Danjaq's permission, was preparing to authorize Pathé to broadcast Bond films on television in multiple countries. These complicated legal disputes put the production of the new film on hold for several years.

27. "Untraceable" (2008)

When producers Tom Rosenberg and Gary Lucchesi read a script called "Untraceable" created by Robert Fyvolent and Mark Brinker, such a timely story immediately broke through their defenses. Lucchesi said: "The FBI's cybercrime investigation unit hasn't been around for very long, just six or seven years. But 'Untraceable' focuses on a very different kind of case, because it has nothing to do with crimes against children or credit card theft. It's about a murderer who is using the Internet to make everything easier to carry out—netizens are drawn to it; the more people log on, the faster the victim dies."

28. "Deja Vu" (2006)

"Deja Vu" is an American science fiction mystery film first released on November 22, 2006. The title, in French, means "time goes back seventy years". The film's plot posits the existence of parallel universes. When a person experiences a time reversal of seventy years, it is because another self in a parallel universe has died, leading to a merging of consciousness.

29. "One Point O" (2004)

Simon is a computer programmer. One morning he wakes up to find a plain brown package in his apartment. He opens it, but there is nothing inside. Although he tries to lock his apartment securely, the mysterious packages keep appearing. Watching his movements through security cameras, Simon gradually becomes neurotic and begins to suspect everyone in the apartment. When one of them suddenly dies, Simon is pushed to the edge of danger. In this mental torment, he frantically searches for what the mysterious force that has changed his life really means.
