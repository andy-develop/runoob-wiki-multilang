Blockchain-related Questions and Analysis

Category Blockchain

Related Technical Points

Q: What is blockchain?

A: Blockchainrefers to a technical solution that collectively maintains a reliable database throughdecentralization and trustlessness. In layman's terms, blockchain technology refers to a way in which everyone participates in keeping the ledger. Every system has a database behind it. You can think of the database as a big ledger. So who keeps the ledger becomes very important. Currently, whoever owns the system keeps the ledger. WeChat's ledger is kept by Tencent, and Taobao's ledger is kept by Alibaba. But in the blockchain system, everyone in the system has the opportunity to participate in keeping the ledger. If there is any data change within a certain period, everyone in the system can keep the ledger. The system will judge who kept the ledger fastest and best during that period, write the recorded content into the ledger, and send the ledger content of this period to all other people in the system for backup. Thus, everyone in the system has a complete ledger. We call this method blockchain technology.

Q: What are the benefits of a blockchain where everyone participates in keeping the ledger?

A:It can be found that at the cost of sacrificing some efficiency, it gainsgreat security. First, there is no central ledger, so it cannot be destroyed. Each node is only part of the system, and each node has equal rights and has exactly the same ledger. Destroying some nodes has no effect on the system at all. Second,cheating is impossible, because unless you can control the computers of most people in the system to make modifications, the system will refer to the opinions of the majority to determine what the real result is. As a result, you will find that modifying your own ledger is completely meaningless (because others do not recognize it). In addition, since there is no centralized intermediary, everything runs automatically through pre-set programs, which can not only greatly reduce costs but also improve efficiency. And because everyone has the same ledger, it can ensure that the ledger recording process is open and transparent.

Q: What problems does blockchain solve?

A:The most important thing blockchain solves is theintermediary creditproblem. In the past, it was difficult for two people who did not know or trust each other to cooperate, and they had to rely on a third party. For example, in payment behavior, any kind of transfer in the past required an institution like a bank or Alipay. But through blockchain technology, Bitcoin is the first time in human history to complete a transfer that both parties can trust without the participation of any intermediary institution. This is a major breakthrough of blockchain.

Q: Is blockchain Bitcoin? Or is Bitcoin blockchain?

A:Blockchain technology is the underlying technology of Bitcoin. In the early days, not many people paid attention to Bitcoin's underlying technology. But when Bitcoin ran very stably for many years without any centralized operation and management, and never had any problems, many people noticed that the underlying technology might have great mechanisms, and it might not only be used in Bitcoin, but perhaps in many fields. So they abstracted and extracted Bitcoin's technology and called it blockchain technology, or distributed ledger technology. So from a certain perspective, Bitcoin can be seen as the first application of blockchain, while blockchain is more like an underlying technology such as TCP/IP, which will expand to more and more industries in the future.

Q: In which industries can blockchain technology mainly be used?

A:The main advantages of blockchain are no need for intermediary participation, efficient and transparent process with low cost, and high data security. Therefore, any industry with a need in any of these three areas has the opportunity to use blockchain technology.

Q: Why should blockchain technology be used in the financial sector? What are the substantial benefits?

A:The main advantages of blockchain technology in the financial field are disintermediation and greatly reduced costs.

First, the financial industry currently needs to conduct layer-by-layer audits to control financial risks in order to prevent single-point failures and systemic risks, but this also creates high internal costs. In addition, due to the emergence of increasing regulations, especially the 2008 financial crisis, the threshold for financial control has been continuously raised, and the war on terror has expanded the scope of anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing, gradually expanding the breadth and depth of regulation, causing the regulatory costs of the entire financial system to increase sharply. Under this situation, blockchain technology can greatly reduce the cost of the entire financial system through tamper-proof and highly transparent methods. According to a report released by Santander, the largest bank in Spain, if banks around the world used blockchain technology internally around 2020, they could save about $20 billion in costs each year. Such data is enough to show the huge transformation and breakthrough that "blockchain" brings to the traditional financial field.

In addition, due to historical reasons, traditional financial institutions rely on central clearing houses for settlement and clearing, which causes low efficiency. Traditional cross-border settlement has to go through institutions like SWIFT, so international wire transfers are often calculated in days. But Bitcoin, using blockchain technology, has operated perfectly for seven years without any centralized operating institution, not only achieving real-time settlement and clearing, but also without a single accounting error. Therefore, if all financial systems can achieve decentralized real-time settlement and clearing, it will not only greatly improve global financial efficiency, but also change the global financial landscape.

Q: What is Bitcoin's so-called"mining"?？

A:"Mining" in Bitcoin is actually the process of keeping the ledger. Bitcoin's computation uses a mechanism called "Proof of Work (PoW)". In order to find out who has more powerful computing ability, the system will pose a math problem each time, and only the computer that solves the problem fastest can keep the ledger. The computer that seizes the right to keep the ledger will be rewarded with 25 Bitcoins. This behavior is usually called "mining", and the Bitcoins obtained are regarded as the reward for successful mining.

Q: Does all blockchain need mining?

A:Not all blockchain projects adopt a "Proof of Work" method like Bitcoin. This is more seen in early blockchain projects. If other proof mechanisms are adopted, such as "Proof of Stake (PoS)" and "Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS)", such mining methods are not needed.

Q: What is the relationship between blockchain and big data? Will blockchain replace big data?

A:Blockchain and big data do not have much to do with each other. Big data is mainly about managing massive data, while the core of blockchain is to achieve high security and high reliability of data without the involvement of centralized intermediaries. Therefore, blockchain and big data are not in conflict with each other, nor will they replace each other. They are completely different solutions for data in different scenarios.

Q: What is the relationship between blockchain and cloud computing/cloud storage? Is blockchain cloud computing or cloud storage?

A:Cloud computing is usually defined as providing dynamically scalable and often virtualized resources through the Internet, but the platform providing cloud computing is often a centralized institution. The network composed by blockchain generally has no specific institution, so blockchain is closer to the definition of a distributed computing system and belongs to a type of distributed computing. However, blockchain can implement cloud storage. Unlike the current centralized provision of cloud storage space, there are some blockchain projects that provide decentralized cloud storage solutions. Such projects include Storj, Sia, and Maidsafe.

Q: Is blockchain software? What program is it written in?

A:Blockchain is not a specific piece of software. Just as the word "database" suggests, it is a design concept for a specific technology. It can be implemented in most languages, and there are many ways to implement it. Moreover, blockchain technology is still developing rapidly. Relatively speaking, the current design concept of blockchain technology is still relatively simple, but it may become increasingly complex in the future.

Q: What is a public chain? What is a private chain? What is a consortium chain?

A: Public chainIt is that any node is open to anyone, everyone can participate in this blockchain and contribute computing power, and anyone can download and obtain the complete blockchain data (the entire ledger). However, in some blockchain application scenarios, it is not desirable for anyone to participate in the system or for anyone to view all data. Only authorized nodes can participate and view all data. This kind of blockchain structure is calledprivate chain。

A consortium blockchain means that the permissions of each participating node are completely equal, and everyone can achieve trusted data exchange without needing complete mutual trust. The banking blockchain consortium formed by R3 is building a typicalconsortium blockchain。

However, with the rapid development of blockchain technology, it is not ruled out that the boundary between public chains and private chains will become blurred in the future. Because each node can have relatively complex read and write permissions, perhaps some nodes with partial permissions will be open to everyone, while some nodes with accounting or core permissions can only be opened to authorized nodes. Then it will no longer be a pure public chain or private chain.

Q: What are the main problems in the development of blockchain technology?

A:Currently, blockchain technology is still at a very early stage. Not only has a unified technical standard not yet been formed, but various technical solutions are still developing rapidly. However, problems that were previously considered to be associated with blockchain-based systems, such as being very resource-intensive (similar to Bitcoin) or having data processing limitations, have already achieved technical breakthroughs. But the scalability of blockchain technology has not yet been tested by large-scale practical application, and it is still mainly at the prototype design stage.

If we cannot quantitatively analyze the actual benefits that blockchain technology can bring us, including the funds it can save and the value it can create, then the financial industry will remain relatively cautious in the short term. After all, the current global financial infrastructure investment has exceeded several trillion. To establish a brand-new financial architecture and underlying operating system, it needs to be supported by actual data. In the absence of deployment of existing technology and obtaining use cases, the total amount that can be saved is still very difficult to determine. Up to now, there remains a huge question: exactly how much funding is needed to build a sufficiently powerful blockchain platform to handle the trillions of dollars that the capital market ecosystem faces every day.

In addition, the blockchain industry is extremely short of talent,There is a great shortage of versatile talent who understand both blockchain technology and finance. The market is desperately looking for talents who can connect the two worlds, who can realize blockchain technology in the capital market in the real world and achieve better functionality. Building a brand-new system based on blockchain technology will inevitably require such cross-disciplinary talent.

Q: What is a smart contract?

A: A smart contractis a contract that uses computer language instead of legal language to record terms. A smart contract can be automatically executed by a computing system. If blockchain is a database, then smart contracts are the application layer that enables blockchain technology to be applied to reality. Traditional contracts generally have no direct connection with the computer code that executes the contract content. Paper contracts are archived in most cases, while software executes contract terms written in the form of computer code. The potential benefits of smart contracts include reducing costs in contracting, execution, and supervision; therefore, for many contracts related to low-value transactions, this greatly reduces labor costs.

Q: How are smart contracts used?

A:If the central bank can issue fiat currency through blockchain, it can also use smart contract technology to embed code into the issuance of fiat currency. Then this part of the fiat currency can be called"programmable money". For example, if the central bank designates a certain portion of funds to be issued to agriculture-related accounts, then corresponding programs can be written into this portion of funds, specifying that it can only enter agriculture-related accounts. Under no circumstances can this portion of funds be diverted to other accounts. If most currencies become "programmable money," then we can imagine that the financial environment they form would become"programmable finance"。

Q: What does blockchain have to do with ordinary people?

A:Basically it has nothing to do with them, unless they plan to start a business in this field. It is like the relationship between the TCP/IP protocol and ordinary people. Ordinary people do not need to know what the underlying TCP/IP protocol of the Internet is; they just need to enjoy the services provided by the Internet.

Q: Does a blockchain project necessarily need some kind of coin?

A:No. Bitcoin itself is a payment system, so it needs a tool for value measurement, and therefore bitcoin must appear. In addition, in order to reward more people who are willing to contribute their computers to provide computation for the system, bitcoin is needed for rewards. In some private chain systems, specialized assets can be designed for trading, and every node must participate in computation; this is both their responsibility and their right. Therefore, there is no need to consider encouraging their participation through rewards. Thus, in such systems, it may not be necessary to design some kind of coin.

Q: Is Bitcoin legal now?

A:Bitcoin has always been completely legal in major countries around the world, including China. Due to the misleading of some irresponsible media, many people think that China once declared Bitcoin illegal. In fact, according to the Notice on Preventing Risks of Bitcoin issued by the People's Bank of China and four other ministries on December 5, 2013, it is clearly stipulated that Bitcoin is a specific virtual commodity, and ordinary people have the freedom to participate in it on the premise of bearing their own risks. Various financial institutions and payment institutions are not allowed to carry out Bitcoin-related financial services or use Bitcoin as an investment target. In Germany, Bitcoin is used as a monetary unit; in the United States, it is defined as a commodity. The European Court of Justice considers Bitcoin a means of payment and is not subject to value-added tax.

Q: Can XX coin be invested in? Is it a blockchain project? Is it a pyramid scheme?

A:At present, all digital currencies, including Bitcoin, carry high risks. Blockchain technology itself is in its infancy, and all blockchain projects also carry very high risks. It is not recommended that any ordinary person invest in any digital currency or blockchain-related project. Moreover, digital currency and blockchain have a certain technical threshold, and ordinary people cannot distinguish which projects are real and which are pyramid schemes. Therefore, it is recommended that ordinary people do not invest in any such projects. For any project where you cannot tell whether it is a pyramid scheme, please treat it directly as a pyramid scheme.

Q: How to invest in blockchain?

A:Most blockchains are in the early stages and are mainly overseas. There are very, very few good blockchain projects domestically, so it is not recommended that any non-professionals invest in blockchain projects. If you are very interested in blockchain technology and have a technical or financial background, it is suggested that you consider starting a business in this field.

Q: Who actually invented blockchain/Bitcoin? Is Satoshi Nakamoto Japanese? Is it a conspiracy of the US government?

A:Bitcoin was created by a person or team calling themselves "Satoshi Nakamoto," and they completely withdrew from the project in its early stages. The possibility that "Satoshi Nakamoto" is Japanese is very small, because from his past emails it can be inferred that he should be a native English speaker. In addition, the creator of Bitcoin no longer has any influence on the current Bitcoin project, so it is unlikely to be the product of some conspiracy. Whether "Satoshi Nakamoto" appears later or is physically eliminated, it cannot have too much impact on Bitcoin.

Q: What exactly is the difference between Bitcoin and Q Coin?

A:Q Coin is a centralized electronic currency; its total supply and issuance method are controlled by Tencent. Bitcoin's total supply and issuance method are pre-set by programs and encryption algorithms and then run on multiple nodes around the world. No one or institution can modify them, and they are not controlled by any single person or institution. Q Coin is generally called electronic currency or corporate token. Bitcoin is called digital currency or cryptocurrency.

Q: Is there an upper limit to the total amount of Bitcoin? How is it distributed?

A:As mentioned earlier, miners who compete for the right to keep records have the opportunity to receive rewards. At the beginning, the system rewards the person who records the fastest and best with 50 bitcoins every 10 minutes, and then this 50 halves every four years. Aroundthe year 2140there will no longer be new bitcoins created, and the upper limit of 21 million will be reached. After that, transaction fees will be used to reward miners.
